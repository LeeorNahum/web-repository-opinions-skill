# Test Identities

A product needs identities that are not a person: one for reviewers, one for automated testing once a test has to sign in or read mail, and scoped service credentials for provider administration. One reviewer identity serves every store and platform unless their reviews would collide in one library, and only then does each platform get its own. Create an identity when something needs it, not ahead of time: an address with no consumer is one more thing to route, verify, and retire. Each identity is three separate things. The address is what the product sees. The inbox is where its mail lands. The account is what signs in. Keep them apart in naming, in storage, and in cleanup.

- Name an address by purpose and consumer on the product's own domain, `purpose-consumer@product-domain`, with an environment suffix when stages differ: a testing agent, a reviewer for a given platform, a staging variant of either. No personal names, no model names, and no plus-address on a person's inbox, which ties the identity to that person's mailbox and cannot be handed over.
- Route each address to an inbox the team controls, on the product's domain mail. An automated test that has to read mail gets its own isolated inbox, never a person's.
- Give each identity ordinary product permissions, synthetic sample data, and its own workspace or library, so a reviewer sees what a new user sees and a test never touches real data.
- A reviewer identity signs in with a password and nothing the reviewer cannot complete on a device nobody has seen: no second factor, no device challenge, no code sent to a mailbox they do not hold. Where the auth provider enforces a new-device check, decide at the product level whether to turn it off, exempt the identity, or give the reviewer a sign-in that does not trigger it, and record the decision with its blast radius. Never relay a verification code to a reviewer by hand.
- Provider administration uses scoped service credentials with the least access the job needs, never a person's login.
- Store every credential outside the repository where the next agent can retrieve it, with what the identity is for, when its sign-in was last verified from a fresh device, and when it rotates. Verify the reviewer sign-in again before every submission. A successful sign-in from the owner's account proves nothing about the reviewer's.
- When a review closes, disable or remove its identity. Rotate what stays.
